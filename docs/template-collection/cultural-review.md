# Cultural review and asset decisions

This is an original, illustrative collection inspired by the user's named traditions. It does not reproduce a historical artifact or claim to be an authoritative ceremonial design. Template choice is explicit. No identity field is consulted by discovery or presentation code.

## Reference checks

- [The Met: Sari (Patola)](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/456962) informed the distinction between textile structure and a generic floral border. The generated band is an original geometric interpretation.
- [National Gallery of Australia: Foundation report](https://nga.gov.au/media/dd/documents/foundation_06-07.3d14d3e.pdf) describes Phulkari as embroidered textiles from Punjab and neighboring areas. Our pack uses restrained stitch-like geometry without claiming a specific traditional ceremonial pattern.
- [The Met: Krishna Dancing](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/58449) provides a primary-source example of flute-playing Krishna in miniature painting. Our figure is original, isolated and small, with no copied composition.
- [British Museum: Ganapati painting](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_1992-0410-0-1-41) documents a seated four-armed representation. Four intentional arms are not treated as an AI anatomy defect; malformed attachment or extra unintended limbs are rejected.
- [Unicode Gurmukhi chart](https://www.unicode.org/charts/nameslist/n_0A00.html) identifies ੴ at U+0A74. It is typeset with Noto Sans Gurmukhi, never generated into an image.
- [Unicode Miscellaneous Symbols](https://www.unicode.org/charts/nameslist/n_2600.html) identifies ☬ (U+262C, Khanda) and ☸ (U+2638, Wheel of Dharma). Optional symbols use the verified Noto Sans Symbols glyphs. The companion Symbols 2 font did not contain these glyphs and is not used for them.
- [Noto's usage guidance](https://github.com/notofonts/noto-docs/blob/main/docs/website/use.md) supports explicit script-family fallback. Font source URLs, versions and OFL licenses are recorded locally.

## Review checklist applied to every generated asset

- No text, pseudo-text, logos or watermark artifacts.
- No accidental faces or unrelated religious symbols in regional geometry.
- Isolated art remains clear at the actual printed size.
- Sacred figures have coherent anatomy, complete outlines and respectful framing.
- No body copy or UI overlaps sacred imagery.
- Symbols are separately typeset, opt-in and constrained by the selected template.
- Islamic artwork remains non-figurative and contains no generated calligraphy.
- Jain Ahimsa deliberately uses non-symbolic architecture/lotus rather than an unverified sacred mark.
- Portrait removal and None choices reclaim space; page two does not repeat the first-page sacred composition.
- Color and grayscale rendering are checked; physical printer proofing and community-member review are not claimed.

## Final review decisions

Batch 1: the first Terracotta Arch output was rejected as too ornate and replaced by a minimal pigment arch. The jasmine branch and Deco corner passed visual review.

Batch 2: leaf, interlaced Kolam-inspired corner, Alpana-inspired ornament, Patola-inspired band, stylized Paithani peacock and miniature pavilion have been individually inspected. No lettering or unrelated sacred marks. The peacock is intentionally textile-like, with a folded tail; the pavilion is a small header element rather than a full scene.

Batch 3 asset review: Krishna has two connected arms, two hands at a single flute, a complete figure and one peacock feather. Rama has two connected arms, a bow/quiver and no battle context. Ganesha was regenerated to reduce ornament and clarify the shorter tusk; the accepted seated figure has four deliberate arms (axe, lotus, blessing, modak), one trunk and coherent hands. Ambedkar's portrait is a dignified recognizable editorial bust with glasses and formal dress, without party insignia or slogans. The other six assets are non-lettered botanical, textile or architectural elements. No generated sacred verses are used. The cross, Ik Onkar, Khanda and Wheel of Dharma are tested font glyphs, selected independently.
